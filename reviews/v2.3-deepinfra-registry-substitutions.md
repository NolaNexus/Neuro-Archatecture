# DeepInfra Substitutions for the Appendix D Registry (v2.3)

**Document kind:** registry audit, input only. Not a review, not a revision of the paper.
**Paper version:** v2.3, Appendix D frozen at the 2026-11-07 registry snapshot.
**Catalog snapshot:** DeepInfra public model list (`/v1/openai/models`), read 2026-11-07, 178 entries.
**Question:** for each Ollama entry in Appendix D, is there a DeepInfra-hosted substitute, and what kind of substitute is it?

## The boundary this audit cannot cross

A DeepInfra endpoint is **paid remote inference**, not a resident. Under Law 1 it is an emergency ration requiring a recorded operator authorization (§3, 9.8), or a benchmark oracle outside the organism. It cannot supply `model_residency_<model>`, `kv_usage_<lane>`, `ttft_ms`, `tpot_ms`, `bandwidth_gbs_<node>`, or `joules_task` for the named host, so it cannot enter the Appendix F census matrix and cannot answer Q7–Q9. What it can do is pre-screen a model's quality on golden-set items before a 65 GiB pull, and serve as a second backbone for a decorrelation trial only if that trial is priced as paid dispatch. Every row below is therefore a *candidate for the bench*, never a lane.

Prices are per 1M tokens as listed on the snapshot date. They are observations, not pinned constants.

## Findings

1. **Coverage is thin exactly where the registry is distinctive.** Five entries have an exact or same-family remote counterpart (`gpt-oss-120b`, Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, Qwen3-Coder, Gemma 4, phi-4), one is a family successor (MiniMax M3), and one is an embedding substitute. Five have no counterpart (laguna, d1, North mini code, Mellum2, LFM2.5), and two cannot be identified at all (clef, laguna). The local registry's small-code, sparse-12B, and sub-2B lanes are the parts the remote catalog does not stock.
2. **The Nemotron context claim is the load-bearing discrepancy.** The paper's Chronicle candidate is chosen for a reported 1M-token window. The served DeepInfra window is 262,144. That does not settle the local runtime's window, but it shifts the burden: D.2's "to be verified locally" now has a first external reading against the 1M figure, and the Chronicle role should not be funded until F.4 measures the local KV curve.
3. **A remote substitute cannot answer Q8 or Q9.** Both questions are about thread split and cache eviction on 32 hardware threads. A remote endpoint has no thread share and no warm KV cache on this host, so substituting one into the frontal pair would answer a different question. Q8 now requires installed residents for this reason.
4. **Two configured model IDs are absent from the catalog.** `~/.factory/settings.json` routes to four DeepInfra models. `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017` and `Qwen/Qwen3.8-Max` appear in the snapshot; `Qwen/Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B-Instruct` and `MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-M2.1` do not. Absence from the public list is not proof of removal, but both IDs are documented in `AGENTS.md` and would fail if the endpoint is gone. Check before the next session relies on them.
5. **The paper's Law 1 already prices this correctly.** Nothing here argues for adding a remote column to the lane economy. If v2.4 wants the finding recorded, the right home is a D.5 "remote substitutes (not residents)" note and a Q8 clause, not a residency row.

## What this audit does not do

It does not verify any substitute's quality, KV behavior, or tokenizer against the golden set, and it does not pull weights. It maps names to names on one date. The bench, not this table, decides.
