# Audit: the DeepInfra Route Registry

**Document kind:** audit of a registry amendment proposal. Input only. Not a review of the paper, not a revision.
**Paper version:** v2.3.
**Audited document:** `reviews/v2.3-deepinfra-route-registry.md`.
**Method:** live read of `https://api.deepinfra.com/v1/openai/models` on 2026-11-07, recomputation of every unit cost, cross-reference against §3, §5.3–§5.7, §7.5–§7.6, §9.8, §10, §14 Q8–Q9, Appendices A, B, D, H.
**Finding IDs:** `A#` (audit). Deliberately not `D#`/`F#`/`P#` (v1.2), `N#`/`M#`/`E#` (v1.4), or `R#` (v2.2 round 3), which are owned namespaces.

## 1. Verdict

The document's factual base is sound. Its selection logic is not, and in four places it contradicts the paper it claims to be scoping.

Confirmed against the live catalog: 178 entries, exactly as claimed; all sixteen catalog model ids present; every input, output, and cache price matches to the cent; every unit cost recomputes exactly; the organism total of $0.1398 is exact; the premium multiples of 18×, 37×, and 64× are exact; the minimum viable organism at $0.0195 is exact; the two configured ids named as absent (`Qwen/Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B-Instruct`, `MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-M2.1`) are genuinely absent; the Appendix D.1 measurement battery is quoted verbatim; §3 Law 1 does say paid dispatch is an emergency ration.

What does not hold is the reasoning from those numbers. Three failures are structural rather than arithmetic:

1. **The registry provisions Rung 3 by default**, which §5.6 permits only on evidence and Q8's stated tilt explicitly reverses.
2. **It funds a second 1M-context resident** in Canon while listing "one context-heavy resident, not two" among the findings that survive.
3. **Its unit metric is the wrong instrument for the roles it prices**, so several "why this one" cells justify the opposite of what they assert.

## 3. Findings

### Structural

**A1. Two of eight routes cannot serve the unit they are priced at.** The unit is 150,000 input tokens. Driver (`gpt-oss-120b`) and Surveyor (`granite-4.2-8b`) both serve 131,072. So the $0.1398 organism task is not executable on the registry as written, and the "minimum viable organism (Epoch 5)" inherits the overflow through its Driver half. *Correction:* price against the smallest window on the task path, or state that the 131K routes never see a full unit.

**A2. Canon funds the second context-heavy resident the paper forbids.** §5.7: "it is cheaper to fund one context-heavy resident than to over-provision context on every lane." D.4.2: "fund **one context-heavy resident (Chronicle-class)** and stop there." The registry's own §2 table lists "One context-heavy resident, not two" under **Survives**, then the table in §4 funds two 1M routes. The Canon justification makes it explicit: "Long context lets Canon read its own distillation source without truncation" is precisely the second context-heavy resident. Canon's product is distilled convention, which is compact by definition. *Correction:* Canon at 262K, or restate D.4.2.

**A3. Rung 3 is provisioned by default, which §5.6 does not permit.** §5.6: Rung 3 is "purchased only when workload evidence shows Rungs 1–2 failing," with an automatic promotion clause that reads the band floor. The registry assigns eight distinct vendors at Epoch −1, before any measurement, and calls the frontal pair "already at decorrelation Rung 3." Construction is not evidence. Q8's provisional tilt is the direct opposite: "a same-backbone frontal pair with divergent slices and lateralized instructions should precede any two-heavyweight arrangement." *Correction:* provision Rung 1 for the frontal pair and let promotion fire on the band, or state plainly that Q8's tilt is overturned and on what grounds.

**A4. §5.6 is misread as a cost ordering, and the corrected ordering inverts its own mechanism.** §5.6 opens by rejecting cheap-first ordering in the same paragraph ("Ordered cheap-first ... would put the *least effective* mechanism first") and orders by divergence strength, with promotion driven by measurement. So "the ladder's order should be restated: divergence first" is not a scope note, it is a repeal of the promotion clause. Worse, the stated reason is backwards: "duplicate reads are the only rung that bills" is false on a route. Divergent context slices destroy the shared prompt prefix, so Rung 1 is the rung that *costs* most per token, while Rung 3 (different model ids, identical prompt) adds no per-token cost at all. The conclusion may still be right; the mechanism would propagate into §5.6 as written and should not.

**A5. "Eight families" is a vendor taxonomy, and the catalog cannot support a divergence claim.** Every catalog entry carries `root` equal to its own id and `parent: null`, so there is no lineage field anywhere in the source. "Rung 3 is satisfied by construction" is therefore unsupported by the catalog it cites: an OpenAI id and an NVIDIA id are two vendor names, not two proven weight lineages. Separately, §5.6 requires divergence *within a pair*, not across the organism, so the eight-vendor constraint is stricter than the constitution. It is also what pays for granite over Llama at 3× the read cost. *Correction:* drop the eight-family constraint to eight roles, require divergence per stage only, and record lineage as unverified where it is.

**A6. The selection rationale keeps using the axes §2 declares lost.** §2 marks "active parameters set velocity class" as **Lost** (provider's scheduler) and "residency rent" as **Lost**. §4 then justifies the Driver as "the cheapest 100B-class reasoning route" and the Spotlight by its "35B-A3B" shape. On a route, parameter count and active count are not selection criteria; TTFT/TPOT and price are. The registry should select on the quantities §2 says replace the lost axis.

### Pricing

**A7. Spotlight's justification is backwards.** "Input-cheap for scoped edits with large context; the 0.95 output price is the reason it authors and does not read." Authoring is the output-emitting act (§5.4 rule 2: the leading lens publishes), so the highest output price in the cheap tier is a reason *not* to author with it. On the authoring profile it is the most expensive route in the registry: $0.0136 against $0.0072 for `Qwen3.5-9B` at the same 262K. Either the cell or the slot is wrong.

**A8. One unit metric hides the three cost shapes it is applied to.** Authoring is output-dominated, reading and polling are input-dominated and repeatable, and the Chronicle is cache-dominated. The single 150k/15k mix prices all three identically and therefore cannot distinguish the routes that differ on the axis that matters to the role. The Cartographer is the clearest casualty: §5.5 makes it the repeated re-deriver of the same source artifacts, which is the canonical cache hit, and `gemma-4-26B-A4B-it` is the one route in its tier with **no cache price at all**. On the poll profile `Ling-3.0-flash` is 2.6× cheaper, and `gemma-4-31B-it-turbo` is 11% cheaper at the same 262K. *Correction:* report per-role profiles and state a cache-hit assumption.

**A9. "Cheapest 1M-context route on both cold reads and cache reads (0.015)" is false as written.** 0.015 is the cheapest *cold input* among 1M routes. Cache reads are cheaper on `XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-V2.6-Flash` (0.0028), `DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash` (0.006), and `Qwen3.8-Flash` (0.0141). The choice survives on a blended profile: against MiMo the crossover is at a cache-hit rate of about 87%, so DeepSeek wins below that and loses above it. The sentence should say which assumption it rests on.

**A10. Law 1's denominator is substituted, not transformed, and the ledger cannot close it.** `joules_task` is RAPL-instrumented and `usd_per_joule` is "locality-pinned" to the utility bill. On a route, RAPL measures the client, not the inference, so both lines become unmeasurable rather than transformed. The registry's §2 table labels Law 1's status **Transforms** and substitutes quality per USD, which is a different fitness function. *Correction:* mark `joules_task`, `joules_marginal_task`, and `usd_per_joule` as client-side-only and not attributable, and say plainly that Law 1 is proxied, not preserved.

**A11. Law 2 is lost on all three axes, not the third.** §7 item 2 says "Law 2's third axis is unmeasurable." The registry's own §2 table marks all three lost: velocity class, residency rent, and bandwidth/NUMA. On a route the scarce resources are dollars and rate limits; RAM, threads, and local bandwidth have no referent. The prose understates what the table already says.

### Constitutional

**A12. Law 4's substrate clause loses its referent.** Law 4's operative test is RAM occupancy audited as rent: "a model that occupies RAM and never acts is dead weight wearing a role's name; its residency rent is audited per epoch." A never-selected route occupies nothing and costs nothing, so the §8 under-use alarm still fires (it is route-frequency based, §8) but retirement has no ledger consequence. Law 4 becomes advisory on a route. *Correction:* propose a `route_selection_rate` ledger line and state the consequence rather than offering "budget share," which is zero for exactly the route the alarm is about.

**A13. 9.8 compliance is asserted, not met.** §9.8's guard is "1:1 correspondence between paid ledger rows and recorded operator confirmations"; Appendix A repeats "requires 1:1 operator confirmation record; incident if absent." A standing per-epoch budget envelope is a *delegation* of that confirmation, not a 1:1 record. An eight-role task is eight authoring calls plus eight mate reads, so per-call confirmation is not operationally usable. That means the registry amends 9.8 rather than complying with it, and should say so. *Correction:* state the confirmation unit explicitly, and add the 9.8 amendment to the v2.4 list.

**A14. The six falsifiers are unpowered by Appendix H's standard.** H: "A falsifier is *powered* only when its statistic, data source, decision rule, and power target are all present; otherwise it is registered as unpowered and may not retire an organ." The registry's §6 bullets supply statistics and rules but no power targets and no source column, and the proposed v2.4 list adds no H rows. *Correction:* six H rows with power targets, or label them unpowered.

**A15. The proposed v2.4 change list is incomplete against the repository's own rules.** It omits an Appendix C row, which every version bump requires and which is where a finding must be dispositioned rather than dropped; scope notes in §3, although the document's first argument is that Law 1's local clause is suspended; Appendix A retirements, where `bandwidth_gbs_<node>`, `kv_growth_mib_per_1k`, `model_residency_<model>`, `joules_task`, and `usd_per_joule` all become unfillable, so three additions do not cover five losses; and a re-scope of `ttft_ms`/`tpot_ms`, which already exist "per lane, per prompt class" and need to become per-route rather than being duplicated by new fields.

**A16. The cerebellum paragraph reinstates the defect B17 fixes.** B17: "Epoch 3 currently funds a retrieval index and warm context, a cache. Re-scoped: the organ must predict tool-call and diff outcomes, compare prediction to outcome, and publish the error as a calibration signal." The registry's "Cerebellum (Epoch 3): `BAAI/bge-m3` ... the retrieval index is built once per epoch" is the cache, not the forward model. *Correction:* drop the paragraph or make it a prediction-error organ.

**A17. Fallbacks are required by the document's own falsifier and not supplied.** "Every role needs a declared fallback route, and a fallback is a table entry, not a resident." The table has no fallback column. *Correction:* add one, or demote the sentence to a proposal.

### Registry conflict

**A18. Nemotron's window is 256K served, 1M documented, and the window allocation is inverted.** The registry table's 262,144 matches the route; D.2 records the same model as "reported 1M-token context" and calls it "the registry's one plausible context-heavy resident." The catalog description confirms the capability ("up to 4x higher throughput on a 1M-token context"), so the gap is served-versus-nominal, exactly the distinction §5.7 makes about KV. The consequence is a role mismatch: the longest window in the registry goes to the Sentinel, whose job is a short bid evaluation, while Canon, whose product is compact doctrine, gets a second 1M window. *Correction:* assign the long window to the role that reads long, and record the served ceiling next to the documented one.

### Minor

**A19. "No route clears that bar on a coding task" is asserted, not measured.** The table is a cost ratio; Law 1's fitness is quality per completed, verified task, and cost per *completed* task diverges when the cheap route fails. The "must beat it by 18×" framing is the right test; the conclusion needs a golden-set number or an explicit "unmeasured."

**A20. Three small claims do not survive contact.** "Every route under three cents a call" is false for Canon, at exactly $0.0300. The Cartographer at $0.0156 is not "the floor of the tier" (`gemma-3-12b-it` 0.0097, `Ling-3.0-flash` 0.0117, `granite-4.2-8b` 0.0128). The Reader's "code-family lineage" is unsupported by the catalog description, which says "low-latency, high-concurrency, cost-sensitive" and carries no code tag; `Seed-2.0-code` is the code route, at 5.7× the price.

## 5. Required before v2.4

1. Resolve A1 (unit versus window) and A2 (one 1M resident) as text changes, not footnotes.
2. Decide A3 explicitly: does the route provision Rung 1 and measure, or does it overturn Q8's tilt?
3. Rewrite A4 before any §5.6 scope note, since the current mechanism would invert the ladder's rationale.
4. Replace the single unit with per-role profiles and a stated cache-hit assumption (A8).
5. Add Appendix A retirements, not only additions (A10, A15), plus `route_selection_rate` (A12).
6. Add six Appendix H rows with power targets (A14).
7. Add the Appendix C row and the §3 scope note (A15).
8. Add a fallback column (A17).
9. State the 9.8 confirmation unit (A13).

The registry is worth building. It is not yet an amendment, because in four places it argues against the paper it is amending.
